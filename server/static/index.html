Build your own trebuchet

A trebuchet is a medieval siege machine: a heavy weight falls, swings a long arm, and a sling on the end of the arm flings a stone. This one is desk-sized: you print the parts, add a few screws, a bolt, some string, and it throws a small ball several metres.

How to use this page

  1. Go through the numbered steps on the left. The estimated throw updates as you change things.
  2. Press Build: the 3D model appears with a full report underneath. Press ▶ Throw to watch it swing.
  3. Download the ZIP: the parts to print, a shopping list and the step-by-step build notes.

What makes it throw farther

Counterweight
The energy comes from the weight falling. Double the weight, roughly double the energy. Very heavy weights strain the parts.
Weight ratio
Counterweight ÷ projectile. Above about 100 : 1 the arm can't swing any faster, so extra weight helps less and less.
Arm ratio
Long side ÷ short side. Around 3.5–4.5 : 1 usually works best for a hinged counterweight.
Sling length
A longer sling acts like a longer arm, but too long and it drags. Try 0.7–1.0 × the long side.
Release angle
When the ring slides off the pin. Too early and the shot goes up or backwards; too late and it hits the ground. Bend the pin to tune it; the report suggests an angle and a printed gauge helps you bend it.
Friction
Bearings on the axle and a smooth trough help. A loose, wobbly machine wastes energy.

Key terms

Axle (pivot)
The bolt the arm swings on, held by two A-shaped frames.
Hinged counterweight
The weight hangs from a hinge instead of being fixed to the arm. It falls more straight down, so more of its energy reaches the projectile.
Trough
The flat track the projectile slides along before the sling lifts it.
Release pin
A short bent pin at the tip of the arm. One end of the sling has a ring that slides off it to let go.
Trigger
A pin and cord that hold the arm down until you pull it.
Efficiency
How much of the falling weight's energy ends up in the projectile. Good trebuchets reach 60–75%.

About the estimate

The page simulates the swing: the falling counterweight, the arm, the sling dragging the ball along the trough, the ball swinging free, the release and the flight with air drag. It doesn't model friction, stretchy string or a bendy arm, so real throws land shorter: the report shows the model's number and the range to expect (60–85% of it). Distances are measured from the axle, landing at table height.

Safety

It throws hard enough to hurt. Grown-up supervision, safety glasses, never aim at people or animals, and keep hands out of the arm's swing.
